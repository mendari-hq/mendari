2. What Mendari is, and what it is not

Mendari governs AI agents that make everyday decisions in healthcare operations, such as routing a patient’s message or checking a referral, so that they can be tested and compared. It is a research preview.

  • Not a medical device. Mendari is not a medical device and makes no clinical claims.
  • Not medical advice. Nothing the services show or return is medical advice, a diagnosis or a treatment recommendation.
  • Not for patient care. The hosted services are for evaluation and development. Do not use anything they return to make a decision about a real patient.
  • Not for emergencies. The services are not monitored for emergencies. A patient with an emergency should call 911.
  • Synthetic data. The demos, the example agents, their data and their scores are synthetic. The hosted services are for synthetic data only.
  • Agents are not clinically reviewed unless they say so. An agent is not clinically reviewed, and is not for patient care, unless its Decision Card states that it was reviewed, by whom and for what. A publisher’s type on the Hub (community, verified or institutional) says who publishes an agent. It does not say the agent is clinically validated.
  • Scores are engineering evaluations. A score on synthetic data overstates real-world accuracy. It is a check that the software behaves as written, not clinical validation.

An agent’s outcome (automated, human review, abstained or blocked) is the output of software. You, and the qualified people in your organization, remain responsible for every decision.

6. The registry and the Hub

The registry keeps signed agent archives with versions that never change, and the Hub is the website that shows them. This section applies once they are open.

  • Publishing. You publish with a publisher token that belongs to you and your organization, to a namespace your organization holds. A token ends on its expiry date and can be revoked. A new namespace may wait for a reviewer’s approval before anything in it is listed.
  • What is public. What you publish is public: the agent’s archive and its files, its name, namespace and versions, the licence and risk level you declare, your signing keys’ public halves, your publisher type, and the Decision Card and checks the registry computes from the agent. Publish nothing you are not free to make public, and never patient data.
  • Your licence to others. You publish each agent under the licence its manifest names, and you confirm you have the right to. You also let us store, check, display and distribute it through the registry and the Hub.
  • Versions are permanent. A published version cannot be changed or replaced. You can recall a version you published; a recalled version stops being offered as the latest and stops automating on servers that read the registry, but stays available by its exact version with the recall’s reason.
  • What we may do. We may review an agent, recall it, refuse it, or delete a version that must not be served (for example one that holds patient data, infringes someone’s rights or is unsafe), and we may change or remove a publisher type. The record of each of these acts is kept.
  • The record. Every write to the registry (a publish, a key, a token made or revoked, a review, a recall, a deletion) is added to an append-only record that names who acted. That record is not edited or erased.
  • Using an agent from the registry. A listing is not an endorsement. The registry shows what it checked apart from what the publisher claims. A pulled agent does not automate anything on a Mendari server until that server’s organization approves its exact version, and you must validate it on your own data before any use.
