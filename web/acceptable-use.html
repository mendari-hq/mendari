Acceptable use policy

Draft, pending legal review. Not yet in effect. This text is a working draft for the project’s owner and counsel; it is not legal advice and creates no agreement. Words in brackets are facts still to be supplied.

Last updated 21 November 2026.

This policy is part of the terms of use. It applies to everything we run: this website, the hosted engine, the registry and the Hub, and any sandbox or demonstration. It says what you may not do with them. It does not restrict what you do with the open-source software on your own servers; the software’s licence governs that.

1. No real patient data

  • Enter synthetic data only. Never put a real patient’s name, record, message or details, or any other protected health information, into the hosted engine, a sandbox, a demonstration, the registry or the Hub.
  • Do not connect the hosted services to a health record system that holds real patients’ records.
  • Do not put real patient data, or anyone’s personal information, into an agent, its test cases, a review note, an issue or a pull request.
  • De-identified data is not synthetic data. Use it only where the project’s contribution guide allows: openly licensed and de-identified, in the repository, never typed into the hosted services.

If it happens by mistake, tell us at once (see the terms of use) so it can be erased.

2. Not for patient care

  • Do not use anything the hosted services return to make or support a decision about a real patient.
  • Do not present Mendari, an agent or a score as a medical device, as medical advice, or as clinically validated, unless an agent’s Decision Card states a review and you describe it as the card does.
  • Do not direct patients or the public to the hosted services for care, triage or emergencies.

3. Do not defeat the safety limits

Mendari’s rule is that policy may only lower automation: a limit can send a decision to a person, never the reverse. On the services we run, do not try to get around that. In particular, do not try to:

  • make an agent automate beyond what its risk level allows, or automate an agent your organization has not approved;
  • get around a recall, an automation hold, a review requirement or an approval;
  • disguise an agent’s risk level, intended use or publisher, or publish an agent built to behave differently when it is checked than when it is used;
  • get around the limits on agents from outside authors: their processing budget, their input limits, and the rule that community agents recommend and never automate;
  • alter, forge or erase audit records, signatures or the registry’s record;
  • get around rate limits, daily budgets or quotas, for example with many accounts or keys.

4. No abuse of the services

Do not:

  • break the law, or infringe or misuse anyone’s rights, with the services;
  • reach, or try to reach, accounts, organizations or data that are not yours;
  • share credentials outside your organization, use someone else’s, or sign in as someone you are not;
  • overload or disrupt the services, or probe or scan them beyond what section 6 allows;
  • upload malware, or content meant to attack the people or systems that read it;
  • use the services to send spam, to harass anyone, or to build a model or data set from other people’s information;
  • resell the hosted services or offer them to others as your own.

5. Publishing agents

When you publish to the registry, or contribute an agent to the project:

  • publish only what you have the right to publish, under the licence your agent names;
  • do not copy proprietary clinical criteria, payer policies, or licensed questionnaires and scoring instruments, and use code systems that have licence terms only as those terms allow;
  • describe the agent honestly: its intended use, its risk level, who reviewed it and what it was not reviewed for;
  • do not claim a namespace or a name to pass as someone else;
  • recall a version promptly when you learn it is unsafe or wrong.

6. Security research and reporting

We welcome reports of security problems, and they get priority.

  • How to report. Privately, through GitHub’s private vulnerability reporting on the project’s repository: report a vulnerability. Never in a public issue, pull request or discussion. The project has no security e-mail address. The same contact is in this site’s security.txt.
  • What to expect. The project’s security policy says: an acknowledgement within 3 working days, an aim to fix critical issues within 14 days, credit if you want it, and coordinated disclosure within 90 days.
  • How to test. Test on your own copy: the software is open source and runs locally. Do not test against deployments you do not own, never use real patient data, and do not test by volume (denial of service is out of scope).
  • Clinical content. An agent whose clinical content is wrong is not a security report: open a normal issue, since it needs a clinician’s review.

7. What happens if this policy is broken

We may remove content, recall or delete an agent, revoke keys and tokens, and suspend or end access, as the terms of use describe. Where there is a risk to patients or to other users we may act first and explain afterwards. To report a breach of this policy that is not a security problem, write to [contact address for legal questions].
